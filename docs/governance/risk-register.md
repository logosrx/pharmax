This register is the standing list of identified risks to Pharmax, the current controls that address them, the residual rating, and the named owner. It is maintained continuously and reviewed formally during the [annual risk assessment](./risk-assessment-procedure.md). Each entry maps to one or more controls in [`../security/control-matrix.md`](../security/control-matrix.md) and, where the residual rating is composite-16 or higher, to an active mitigation plan referenced in the entry.

## Scoring

| Axis       | 1                                                      | 2                                                                | 3                                                                  | 4                                                              | 5                                                                                                |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Likelihood | Negligible — has not happened in the industry recently | Low — possible in the industry, unlikely against Pharmax in 12 m | Moderate — known events in the industry; possible against Pharmax  | High — recurring industry pattern; plausible against Pharmax   | Very high — recurring against products of our shape; expected if controls degrade                |
| Impact     | Trivial — operational nuisance, no customer impact     | Low — single-customer minor disruption                           | Moderate — multi-customer disruption or single-tenant PHI exposure | Significant — multi-tenant PHI exposure, regulatory engagement | Severe — wide PHI breach, existential reputational damage, regulatory action against the company |

Composite = likelihood × impact. The triage threshold is **composite ≥ 16** — those entries carry an active mitigation plan. Composite 8–15 are monitored and addressed in the next planning cycle. Composite ≤ 7 are accepted with the current controls and re-checked at the annual review.

### R-004 — Operator account takeover

> **Rescoped 2026-08-15 (was "Clerk account takeover").** Clerk was retired by [ADR-0030](../adr/0030-in-house-identity-engine.md) (Accepted 2026-07-10), which supersedes ADR-0015; authentication is now the in-house `@pharmax/auth` engine. The risk is unchanged in substance — an operator credential is compromised and the attacker gets that operator's role — so the entry keeps its number and history rather than being closed and re-opened. What changed is that the controls are now ours to implement and to evidence, which is a net increase in our obligations, not a decrease. **Be precise about what "retired" means here, because "Clerk is retired" full stop would be the same class of error this entry was corrected for.** Clerk is no longer the authentication provider — no `@clerk/*` package is a dependency in any `package.json`, and no Clerk webhook route exists in `apps/web`. What remains, all verified in code 2026-08-15, is in three categories. **(1) Dormant schema:** `user.clerkUserId` (nullable, partial-unique) plus the `ClerkWebhookEvent` model and `ClerkWebhookEventStatus` enum in `prisma/schema.prisma`. The only remaining writer of the column is the dev-only `scripts/dev-link-clerk-operator.ts`. **(2) Live tooling that still reads it:** `apps/worker/src/compliance/access-review-job.ts` selects `clerkUserId`, and `access-review-renderer.ts:182` emits an access-review checklist line asserting that system identities carry no `clerkUserId` (per §5.3.6); `packages/security/src/access-review/generate-access-review.ts` and `scripts/soc2/export-user-roster.ts` likewise. These are harmless while the column is uniformly null, but they are load-bearing compliance output, so they must be re-pointed and not merely deleted. **(3) Artefacts that still describe Clerk as the _current_ provider — the actionable set:** `scripts/soc2/export-clerk-session-log.ts` is a SOC 2 evidence script cited as primary evidence for **CC6.1-1 and CC6.5-1** whose header states that "Clerk owns the session surface (ADR-0015)" and which exports the `clerk_webhook_event` table — a table nothing writes any more, so the artefact would present as an empty or frozen slice while still being offered as evidence for two controls; `packages/orgs/src/commands/invite-user.ts:11-17` documents the live invite flow as "Clerk handles the auth flow" / "Teammate signs in via Clerk"; `packages/tenancy/src/tenant-scoped-models.ts:328-334` points at `apps/web/src/server/auth/clerk-webhook-handlers.ts`, which no longer exists; `apps/web/src/components/shell/auth-shell.tsx:13` still exports a `clerkAppearance` theme for widgets that are gone; and `infra/terraform/modules/ecs/main.tf:141-143` still injects `CLERK_SECRET_KEY`, `CLERK_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, and `NEXT_PUBLIC_CLERK_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` into the ECS task from Secrets Manager. ## Maintenance

- The CTO is the named owner for every entry. As the team grows, ownership rotates to the role best positioned to monitor and address the risk.
- New risks identified during incidents, drills, or vendor reviews are added here within five business days of identification.
- Risks are not removed; they are closed with a residual rating change and the reason. A closed entry remains for the audit trail.
- The annual risk assessment ([`risk-assessment-procedure.md`](./risk-assessment-procedure.md)) is the formal re-rating pass.
- Dismissing a code-scanning alert requires naming it under R-022 with the reason it was dismissed. A justification that lives only in the GitHub UI is not discoverable during a review pass.
